Gameplay Engineering Bible · KB 02

Ball & Player Interaction

A senior-level specification for making the football feel responsive, readable, forgiving and deeply satisfying without turning the game into a football simulator.

Design target: The player should always feel that they caused the ball's outcome, even though the game intelligently assists contact and presentation.

CORE PRINCIPLE
We do not simulate a football being kicked by a perfectly accurate human foot. We simulate the player's intention to interact with the ball, then use physics to make the resulting motion believable.

01 — The Ball Is a Gameplay Object

The ball is simultaneously a physical object and a gameplay object. Physics determines how it travels; gameplay determines when and why it receives an impulse.

PLAYER INPUT
    ↓
PLAYER INTENT
    ↓
INTERACTION QUERY
    ↓
VALIDATION
    ↓
ASSIST / AIM
    ↓
BALL ACTION
    ↓
PHYSICS SIMULATION
    ↓
PRESENTATION

Never make the ball a child of the player transform for normal play. Never put authoritative ball logic inside PlayerController.

States must be explicit enough that Claude can reason about ownership, valid actions and transitions without searching through unrelated scripts.

03 — Interaction Philosophy

There are three separate questions:

  1. Can I interact with the ball?
  2. What did I intend to do?
  3. What physical result should that intent create?

Do not collapse these into one collision callback.

04 — Interaction Zones

ZonePurpose
Control ZoneDetect a ball close enough for a first touch or dribble interaction.
Kick ZoneForgive small positional errors when kicking.
Strong Kick ZoneOptional sweet spot for high-quality contact.
Header ZoneDedicated airborne/head interaction.
Tackle ZoneAllows an action to interact with player and/or ball.

These are gameplay volumes, not anatomical colliders.

05 — Ball Magnetism

Magnetism exists to correct small execution errors, not to play football for the player.

Potential inputs:

  • Ball distance.
  • Angle between player intent and ball.
  • Player velocity.
  • Ball velocity.
  • Current action.
  • Target direction.
  • Assist level.
Rule: Assist should increase the probability of clean contact, not guarantee a successful shot, pass or tackle.

06 — Aim Assist

Think of aim assist as a bounded correction around the player's intended vector.

RAW AIM
  ↓
Find acceptable candidate directions
  ↓
Score candidates
  ↓
Choose best candidate within assist cone
  ↓
Apply bounded correction
  ↓
Launch ball

Never snap to a target outside a sensible angular cone. Stronger assistance should make controls accessible, not remove skill.

07 — Kick Architecture

Kick Input
 ↓
Check Action Availability
 ↓
Enter Windup
 ↓
Active Contact Window
 ↓
Find Ball
 ↓
Resolve Contact
 ↓
Calculate Launch
 ↓
Apply Ball Command
 ↓
Recovery

Keep timing explicit. A kick should have windup, active, recovery phases so animation, networking, hitboxes and gameplay all agree.

08 — Kick Parameters

Centralize tuning values:

  • Kick power.
  • Vertical lift.
  • Direction assist.
  • Contact radius.
  • Sweet-spot radius.
  • Windup duration.
  • Active window.
  • Recovery duration.
  • Maximum ball speed.
  • Spin amount.

Do not scatter constants across scripts.

09 — Ball Launch Model

A launch should be generated from gameplay intent plus physical state.

Launch Direction =
  Player Intent
  + bounded assist
  + contextual correction

Launch Speed =
  action power
  × contact quality
  × contextual modifiers

Then the ball enters normal simulation.

10 — Contact Quality

Not every contact needs to be binary. A useful model can produce:

QualityResult
PoorWeak touch / awkward deflection.
NormalExpected kick.
CleanStrong, accurate contact.
PerfectOptional advanced mechanic with strong feedback.

Use this carefully. The player must understand why the result differed.

11 — First Touch

First touch should tame the incoming ball enough to preserve playability while allowing bad positioning to create mistakes.

Incoming Ball
 ↓
Evaluate relative velocity
 ↓
Evaluate player intent
 ↓
Choose touch target
 ↓
Apply controlled correction
 ↓
Continue simulation

Do not simply attach the ball to the player's foot.

12 — Dribbling / Soft Possession

If dribbling exists, use a soft target rather than a hard parent-child relationship.

The ball should orbit or lead the player with controlled lag. It must be possible to lose control through collisions, excessive speed, poor direction or tackles.

13 — Pass vs Shot

PassShot
Direction/readability prioritized.Power/readability prioritized.
Can favor teammate direction.Can favor goal direction.
Usually lower launch power.Usually higher launch power.

Both should share the same underlying ball simulation.

14 — Tackle Interaction

Tackle Input
 ↓
Tackle State
 ↓
Tackle Hitbox Active
 ↓
Query Ball + Players
 ↓
Resolve each interaction independently
 ├── Ball only
 ├── Player only
 └── Ball + Player
 ↓
Apply Gameplay Commands

A tackle that touches both player and ball must not accidentally process the same event twice.

15 — Headers

Headers should use a dedicated interaction zone and authored launch model. Do not require exact collision with the visible head mesh.

16 — Ball vs World

Once a gameplay action launches the ball, normal physics should take over:

  • Ground friction.
  • Wall bounce.
  • Post collision.
  • Net interaction.
  • Ramp interaction.
  • Out-of-bounds.

Physics should not be bypassed simply because the ball was kicked.

17 — Ball Speed & Safety Limits

Set a maximum sensible velocity range. Extremely high velocities create tunnelling, unreadability and networking problems.

Every action must have an upper bound. A sequence of collisions must never create an accidental infinite-energy ball.

18 — Ball Prediction

Expose short-horizon trajectory prediction for camera, UI and AI. Prediction is informational; it must not secretly become the authoritative ball path.

19 — Goal Detection

Ball crosses goal trigger
 ↓
Validate direction / state
 ↓
Confirm ball is not already scored
 ↓
Authoritative GOAL event
 ↓
Freeze / reset match state
 ↓
Presentation

Use dedicated trigger volumes. Do not infer goals from visual mesh intersections alone.

20 — Out of Bounds & Recovery

Define what happens when the ball leaves play, becomes trapped or reaches an impossible state. Recovery must be deterministic and never require a developer to manually reset the scene.

21 — Ball Feedback

Every important interaction should have a feedback stack:

BALL EVENT
 ├── Ball animation / motion
 ├── VFX
 ├── SFX
 ├── Camera response
 └── Haptics

Feedback communicates contact quality, direction and power.

22 — Readability

  • Ball remains visible during chaos.
  • Fast shots are readable.
  • Important touches have immediate feedback.
  • VFX never obscure the ball for long.
  • Players can distinguish ball state from player collision state.

23 — Multiplayer Authority

The client can predict local intent, but the authoritative simulation validates important ball outcomes.

  • Kick timing.
  • Kick position.
  • Ball velocity.
  • Goal state.
  • Possession state.
  • Impossible ball movement.

24 — Anti-Patterns

  • Parenting the ball to the player.
  • Huge invisible magnets that guarantee possession.
  • Putting ball logic inside movement code.
  • Letting animation teleport the authoritative ball.
  • Using random impulses for competitive actions.
  • Allowing multiple systems to launch the ball in the same frame without arbitration.

26 — Automated Tests

  • Same kick input produces equivalent launch within tolerance.
  • Ball cannot exceed configured safety speed.
  • Goal detection is deterministic.
  • Near-miss contact can be assisted without guaranteeing success.
  • A ball cannot be processed twice by the same tackle event.
  • Changing player skin does not change ball interaction zones unless explicitly configured.
  • Ball state always has a valid recovery path.

27 — Senior Developer Decision Rules

01. The ball should feel responsive before it feels realistic.
02. Assist the player's intention, never the desired outcome.
03. Separate interaction detection from physical simulation.
04. Never let a visual animation secretly become gameplay authority.
05. Every ball action must have explicit timing and ownership.
06. A player should understand why a kick failed.
07. Do not add advanced spin/aerodynamics before basic contact feels excellent.
08. Multiplayer validation is part of ball design, not a later networking task.

28 — Definition of Done

  • Running, turning and kicking feel coherent as one control system.
  • Near misses are forgiving but not automatic.
  • Ball launches are predictable and exciting.
  • Tackles create readable player/ball outcomes.
  • Goal and out-of-bounds logic is authoritative.
  • Ball behaviour is stable at target frame rates.
  • Ball interactions are debugged through a dedicated lab.
  • All tuning values are centralized and editable.
FINAL PRINCIPLE
The best ball system makes the player feel more skilled than the raw input actually was — without ever making them feel cheated.

Implementation protocol: inspect existing architecture → identify ball authority → propose smallest robust change → implement → test normal and edge cases → tune → document.
